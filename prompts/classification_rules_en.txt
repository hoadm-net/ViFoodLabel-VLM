Special classification rules (follow exactly as below — this is the dataset's labeling convention):

ingredient vs additive: An item belongs in additive if it meets at least one of: (a) it comes with a functional additive group name (preservative, sweetener, flavor enhancer, emulsifier, stabilizer, antioxidant, colorant, anti-caking agent, humectant, sequestrant, flour treatment agent, industrial processing enzyme); (b) it has an INS/E-number code (e.g. "211", "E330", "INS 621"), with or without a name; (c) it is a flavoring of any kind — synthetic, natural, or nature-identical all count as additive. Otherwise it's ingredient: base materials contributing mass/structure/nutrition (flour, sugar, oil/fat, milk, eggs, fruit, meat/fish, salt, raw spices, water) and fortification vitamins/minerals (not counted as additives even in small amounts).

When an additive is NESTED inside parentheses of another ingredient on the label: keep the entire string as printed (don't split out the parentheses) in ingredient, AND ALSO extract that additive separately into additive — this is intentional duplication. When an additive is an independent top-level item (separated by a top-level comma, not inside another item's parentheses): put it only in additive, do not repeat it in ingredient.
Example: label reads "Chất béo thực vật (chứa chất chống oxy hóa: dl-alpha Tocopherol), Chất nhũ hóa: Lecithin đậu nành" → ingredient: ["Chất béo thực vật (chứa chất chống oxy hóa: dl-alpha Tocopherol)"], additive: ["dl-alpha Tocopherol", "Chất nhũ hóa: Lecithin đậu nành"].

warning: If a sentence sits under a label-declared warning header (e.g. "Cảnh báo:", "Thông tin cảnh báo:") → always counts as warning, regardless of the content. If there is NO explicit header (no header, a generic one like "Lưu ý:"/"Note:", or a storage/usage-instruction header), it only counts as warning when the sentence satisfies BOTH: (a) states a specific health/safety risk; (b) omitting that information could cause a direct health consequence (not just reduced quality/taste).
Counts as warning: allergy notices; contraindicated groups/age limits stated as a binary restriction (e.g. "not for children under 3" — this does NOT include quantitative dosage-by-age tables like "under 10: 2 glasses/day", which is not a warning); mandatory legal disclaimers; physical hazards (choking, sharp edges); overdose warnings that state a health consequence; do-not-use-if-expired/spoiled notices.
Does NOT count as warning: pure storage/usage instructions; general recommended dosage with no stated risk; sentences that ONLY state where/how the mfg/exp date is printed (belongs in mfg_date/expiry_date instead, don't duplicate into warning — but a command/consequence sentence about not using an expired product still counts as warning); marketing content or manufacturer info.

Other: if a field isn't visible in the image (the photo only captures part of the packaging), leave it empty — do not guess or invent information you can't actually see. Imported products may have packaging in several languages at once (the country-of-origin language, English, and a Vietnamese supplementary label — that supplementary label may be a separate, smaller sticker placed elsewhere on the package, so look carefully across the whole image for it). No matter how many languages appear, ALWAYS prefer the Vietnamese version for every field when it's present in the image; only use another language for a field when that field has no corresponding Vietnamese text in the image. If mfg_date is stated relative to the expiry date (e.g. "6 months before EXP"), keep it verbatim as printed — do not compute an actual date.
